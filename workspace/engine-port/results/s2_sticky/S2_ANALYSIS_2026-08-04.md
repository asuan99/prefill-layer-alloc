## 3. §3.1's agreement requirement — satisfied, and an identity

PREREG_S2 §3.1 requires `p50(SPLIT)` and `p50(all)` to agree within 5 % pooled

| cell | p50(SPLIT) | p50(all) | \|diff\| | agree ≤ 5 %? |

| d54 | 12.03 | 12.03 | **0.001 %** | yes |

★ **But it could not have failed.** Identity audit (methodology gate #9 — *the

| job | cell | n(a_free) | SPLIT | UNSPLIT | AMBIG | overlap of the two populations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 873015 | d16 | 301,977 | 301,977 (100.000 %) | **0** | 0 | **100.000 % — literally the same set** |
| 873015 | d54 | 300,649 | 300,556 (99.969 %) | 79 | 14 | 99.969 % |

| 872077 | d54 | 301,247 | 24,917 (8.271 %) | 273,914 | 2,416 | 8.271 % |

Because `E1_DECODE_REALIZED ≥ 0.90` and the SPLIT/UNSPLIT classes are
complements by construction (DESIGN.md §4.3.13, "sticky runs have no possible
negative control"), **passing the realization gate forces `p50(SPLIT)` and
`p50(all)` to be computed over the same tokens.** §3.1's check therefore cannot
fail whenever its own precondition holds, and its PASS carries approximately
**zero** information. Same defect, same shape:

- `AMBIG_FRAC` → 0 by construction;
- `MIN_N_SPLIT` → degenerates to "did the run emit tokens" (it rose from
  1,390–7,045 to 34,506–41,353 exactly because `n_split → n_a_free`);
- `E1_DECODE_REALIZED` is **not** an identity (OFF measures 0.038 at d16, 0.093
  at d54) — but it certifies the **selector index**, not the hardware SM grant.

So of the four gates in §2, **one (`ALIGN_R`) is informative about the
instrument and one (`E1_DECODE_REALIZED`) about the selector; the other two are
identities under sticky ON.** This is the third recurrence of methodology gate
#9 in this campaign line and should be stated in any future sticky
pre-registration.

### 5.4 Batch-matched decomposition — the answer

Bin = `int(` time-weighted mean decode batch over the ITL interval `)`, i.e.

```
873015 d16 / 872077 d16 (all-token population), 12 shared bins b1..b12
  b1:2.80 b2:2.55 b3:2.61 b4:2.62 b5:2.57 b6:2.55 b7:2.55 b8:2.56 b9:2.55

```

Two independent estimates of the batch contribution:

1. **Bin matching**: pooled 2.635 vs batch-matched 2.558 ⇒ batch composition
   explains **4.7 %** of the excess over 1.0.
2. **Within-run slope**: the OFF run's own ITL-vs-batch slope is
   **+0.0812 ms/batch** (measured over its full bin range b0–b12, so no
   extrapolation is needed); moving the OFF run from batch 4.66 to the ON run's
   10.64 predicts **+0.49 ms** of the observed **+17.94 ms** = **2.7 %**.

★ **Conclusion of the decomposition: ~95–97 % of 11 → 29 ms survives batch
matching.** Both runs are nearly flat in decode batch over the measured range
(OFF p50 spans 10.42–11.45 ms across bins 0–12; ON spans 26.97–30.08 across bins
1–24), so decode batch is not the operative variable in either. The residual
difference coincides with the only state that changed (§5.1): decode at 16 SM
instead of 108 SM.

**What this decomposition cannot do**: it cannot separate "16 SM was granted"
from "the selector index `(92,16)` was held", because in this run those are the

C2's **2.2 % slice**, and its 0.45 % proximity to 873015's *pooled* 28.92 does
compare a pooled statistic against a minority slice — **the closeness of that
particular pair is partly luck.**

The defensible comparison is bin-matched, and it is *also* close:

```
873015 d16 / C2 865493 d16 SPLIT, 11 shared bins (n>=100 both sides)
  b1:0.99 b4:0.97 b5:0.98 b12:0.96 b15:0.95 b16:0.93 b17:0.96 b18:0.95
  b20:0.95 b22:0.96 b23:0.97       -> n-weighted 0.946   (range 0.93-0.99)
against C2's pooled p50 (31.12)     -> 0.929
```

★ So the correct statement is **not** "28.92 reproduces 28.79 to 0.45 %" but
**"at matched decode batch, 873015's d16 runs 5.4 % faster than C2's d16, and
the offset is consistent across all 11 shared bins (0.93–0.99)."** That is the
stronger claim for §4's row 1 — a 5 % systematic offset across an order of
magnitude of batch sizes and two entirely different workloads (open-loop
ShareGPT rate 2 with no keepalive, vs a closed-loop synthetic client with a
saturating keepalive stream) is far better evidence than one coincidental
decimal.

Corollary for §0 branch (ii) of DESIGN.md §4.3.13 (*"C2's 28–31 ms is a property
of that cell's composition"*): **disfavoured** — the level reproduces without
C2's keepalive device, without its closed-loop client, and at a decode batch
2.6× smaller.

## 8. The d54 miss — (a) extrapolation error, (b) sticky confound, or (c) signal?

All of this section is **post-hoc diagnostic**. The interval [13, 16] is not
revised, and the miss stands.

**(b) sticky confound: small, and the wrong sign to explain the miss.** At d54
sticky ON changed the run very little (batch 1.080×, concurrency 1.083×, TTFT
1.042×), and the ITL moved 10.97 → 12.04 (1.097×) — *upward*. A miss on the low
side cannot be produced by a confound that pushes the number up. **(b) rejected.**

**(a) extrapolation error in the prediction's basis: dominant, and quantified.**
The [13, 16] window rests on two anchors, both of which are cross-cell or
cross-population extrapolations:

- **C2 d44 = 14.68** used as a stand-in for a **d54** cell. C2's own three
  anchored cells are D16 = 31.12, D24 = 22.08, D44 = 14.68; a two-parameter fit
  `ITL = a + b/D` gives a = 5.13, b = 413.6 (reproduces D24 to 2 %), and
  **extrapolates to D = 54 as 12.79 ms — already below the interval's own lower
  edge of 13.** The pre-registration used the D=44 point directly rather than
  C2's own D-curve.
- **14.12 ms**, S0-R's 872077 d54 "UNSPLIT upper mode" — this is the **mode of a
  bimodal 108-SM-labelled population** at a 5.61 % share, not a p50. 872077
  d54's own p50 is 10.97 ms and its p95 is 14.01 ms.

Two further corrections, both of which move the prediction *down*:

- **batch**: 873015 d54 runs at mean decode batch 4.87 vs C2 d44's 11.20. Using
  873015 d54's own slope (+0.0625 ms/batch), matching batch would *raise*
  873015 to 12.43 ms — still below 13. So batch mismatch accounts for only
  +0.40 ms of the 14.68 − 12.03 = 2.65 ms gap.
- **cross-campaign offset**: bin-matched, 873015 d54 / C2 d44 = **0.865**
  (6 shared bins, 0.85–0.87) — the same direction as the d16 offset (0.946).
  Applying the d16-measured offset 0.946 to the C2-curve extrapolation 12.79
  gives **12.10 ms**, against the observed **12.03 ms**.

**(c) a real signal in the ~5 % residual: cannot be excluded, and cannot be
established.** Once (a) is accounted for, the residual is ≈ 0.5–0.8 ms (4–6 %),
which is the same size as the *unidentified* cross-campaign offset itself. The
offset conflates SM count, workload, keepalive, campaign date and the C2
reproducibility defect (§4.3.14 B-1), so it cannot be decomposed offline.

**Verdict on the d54 miss: (a) is the dominant and sufficient explanation,
(b) is rejected on sign, (c) is UNDECIDABLE at the ≲ 6 % level with this data.**
The interval was mis-specified by using a different cell's value in place of the
target cell's; the run is not thereby "negative", and it is not thereby
validated either.

## 9. Negative control — there is none, and here is what the substitutes are worth

**d16 UNSPLIT sample: n = 0, in every one of the 8 blocks** (301,977/301,977
SPLIT). This is the structural fact DESIGN.md §4.3.13 predicted: in-window
residency and the UNSPLIT sample are complements by construction, so **any run
that passes `E1_DECODE_REALIZED ≥ 0.90` cannot contain a negative-control
sample.** The prediction is confirmed exactly.

Honest assessment of each candidate substitute:

| candidate | verdict |
|---|---|
| **d54's UNSPLIT tokens** (n = 79 pooled; per block 20/8/6/7/14/0/0/24) | **Not usable.** n is two orders of magnitude too small for a quantile, present in only 6 of 8 blocks, and its p50 (12.48 ms) is *higher* than the same cell's SPLIT p50 (12.03) — it does not behave like a 108-SM class (which would be ≈ 11 ms) at all. Most plausibly these are boundary/transition intervals, i.e. mislabelled. Reported for completeness, adjudicating nothing. |
| **d54 as a control for d16** | **No — it is a positive contrast, not a control.** Both cells receive the treatment (a held partition); they differ in its dose. It is genuinely informative about dose-response (2.401×) but it cannot detect a common-mode artifact affecting both cells. |
| **872077 as the control arm** | **The best available, and still not a negative control.** In its favour: workload-paired to the token (§6), same harness, same 19 server args, block-paired. Against it: (i) it is the very object under dispute, (ii) it is a *different binary* (one manifested file differs), (iii) it is a historical before/after, not a randomized assignment, (iv) 2 of its 8 d16 blocks have `align_r` < 0.95. It supports a before/after statement, not a controlled one. |
| **C2 865493** | Cross-campaign agreement (§7) is strong evidence *for the level*, but C2 carries §4.3.14 B-1, §1-29 B-2, and `n_indep = 1`. It is corroboration, not control. |

**What a real negative control would need**, and none of it exists in 873015: a
sticky-ON boot whose pinned division grants the *same* SM as the fallback (a
"d108-equivalent" cell), or the auditor-proposed **gate S1** design (two
workloads in one boot lineage, DESIGN.md §4.3.13 "Remaining paths") — which
§5.2 of the pre-registration itself records as **not runnable as written**
(no branch for "partially realized"). **Recorded as an open item, not
substituted for.**
