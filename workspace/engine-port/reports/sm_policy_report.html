Four ways to split a GPU's SMs
between prefill and decode

A mechanism-level comparison of fused, co-schedule, agnostic, and layer-aware SM reservation for hybrid SSM+Attention models — grounded in per-layer kernel timings and multi-run serving measurements on A100.

NemotronH-8B · GQA · mamba 24 / mlp 24 / attn 4 Zamba2-2.7B · no-GQA · mamba 45 / hybrid 9 A100-80GB · CUDA13 sglang v0.5.10 · sgl_kernel.spatial
The one relation that governs everything

Splitting SMs between prefill and decode is easy; the catch is granularity. The prefill↔decode SM trade only pays off when the split is held stable for a whole decode step — which is exactly what the uniform pdmux policy (agnostic) already does. Going finer, per layer, was the hypothesis (layer-aware); serving refuted it on all four hybrids — below the step granularity there is no benefit window to capture, only prefill contention to pay, so layer-aware never beats agnostic. The actionable result: use pdmux (agnostic); don't over-starve decode.

02

Why the policies differ: per-layer-type SM sensitivity

The whole question is whether a layer's decode gets slower when you take its SMs away. If yes (compute-bound), you must reserve them; if no (memory-bound), you can release them for free. We measured this per layer type with green-context SM pinning.

NemotronH decode — ms per layer vs decode SMs

batch 32 · ctx≈350 · 210-step mean

mamba (SSD) mlp attn (GQA)

mamba is compute-bound: 0.589 → 1.547 ms/layer as SMs fall 108→16 (2.6×), and it's 62% of the step. attn is flat (memory-bound GQA), but only 4/52 layers. Little is releasable.

Decode step composition — where the 22.5 ms goes

NemotronH · full 108 SM · per decode step

The time-dominant layers (mamba, 62%) are exactly the SM-sensitive ones — so releasing SM would cost decode. The cheap layer (attn, 5%) is the only insensitive one. That misalignment is why layer-aware ≈ agnostic here.

Context flips the story — attn is O(L), mamba is O(1)

full-SM ms/layer vs context length

attn NemotronH (GQA) attn Zamba2 (no-GQA) mamba (either)

Attn decode reads the whole KV each step, so it grows with context — Zamba2's no-GQA attn 7× from ctx348→4092. Mamba's fixed state stays flat. Long context is what makes attn expensive and SM-sensitive.

The SM-sensitivity flip (attn: full vs 16 SM)

ratio of 16-SM latency to full-SM latency

Below 1.0, attn is faster with fewer SMs (memory-bound — release it). Above 1.0 it's slower (compute-bound — reserve it). Zamba2's no-GQA attn crosses hard (4.9× at ctx4092); its mamba goes inverse — literally free to release.

Serving reality — does the micro-benefit survive?

End-to-end goodput@SLO, measured with the async sglang.bench_serving client swept from light load through saturation (NemotronH ~4.3 req/s, Zamba2 ~2.5). NemotronH is the clean high-batch case; Zamba2 is the prefill-bound in3600/out32 case on triton (no CUDA graph), where over-starving decode is fatal — and where the old GIL client's numbers were misleading.

agnostic ≈ layer-aware, both peaking ~2.76 at rate 3 and beating fused as load rises (fused's decode couples to prefill). With only 4 releasable layers on NemotronH, layer-aware adds nothing over uniform reserve — exactly as the decode-side analysis predicts.

Zamba2 — goodput@SLO (async, clean, prefill-bound)

agnostic wins outright (peak 2.07 @ rate 2), while layer-aware collapses to 0 from rate 2 — the worst policy in its own "ideal" regime (45/54 mamba released). The old-client panel had read a layer-aware TTFT edge here; clean async reverses even that (la TTFT p50 5.3s vs agnostic 1.2s @ rate 2). Releasing 45 mamba layers starves an SM-sensitive decode (TPOT 40→131 ms) and backs the queue up. The micro-benefit does not survive.

The honest caveat — and what the clean client corrected

An earlier draft of this panel reported a layer-aware TTFT win and blamed a "170–205 ms TPOT floor" for it not converting to goodput. Both were old-client artifacts: the hand-rolled 60-thread GIL urllib client couldn't drain tokens (inflating TPOT) and it ran past saturation. With the async sglang.bench_serving client, agnostic holds TPOT ≈ 40 ms and delivers real goodput — it's layer-aware and fused whose TPOT blows past 130 ms, not the backend flooring everyone. Absolute TPOT is still higher than a CUDA-graph deploy would show (py3.14 → no graph on pdmux+mamba), but that shifts the numbers, not the ranking: agnostic > fused > layer-aware in this regime.

05

The agnostic_v2 question — size the floor to attention

What if agnostic reserved for the cheap attention layer instead of the SM-hungry mamba — a uniform floor of 16 on every layer? Maximal prefill reclaim, zero protection. Built as a fifth policy and measured with the async sglang.bench_serving client below the ~4.3 req/s saturation point. The answer is unambiguous: it's the worst policy.

NemotronH goodput@SLO — agnostic_v2 collapses

async client · sub-saturation · TTFT≤3s, TPOT≤60ms

fused agnostic v1 agnostic_v2 layer-aware

agnostic_v2 → 0 goodput by rate 3. The others sustain ~2.2–2.8. agnostic v1 and layer-aware track each other (nothing safe to reclaim on NemotronH); agnostic wins the saturating rate. fused fades as its decode couples to prefill.

…because its TPOT explodes past the SLO

median TPOT · the discriminator · red line = 60ms SLO

fused agnostic v1 agnostic_v2 layer-aware

Starving mamba to 16 SMs makes decode 72 → 119 → 191 ms — over the 60ms SLO from rate 2. agnostic v1 stays flat at ~24ms (enough decode SMs). This is exactly the decode-side microbenchmark (§02) showing up in serving.

The rule agnostic_v2 exposes

"Size to attention" bets the SM-sensitive layer survives 16 SMs. On both models it doesn't — NemotronH's mamba and Zamba2's long-context no-GQA attn are both SM-sensitive and time-dominant, so starving them blows up decode. And the freed SMs don't even help prefill: the slow decode backs up and blocks the pipeline, so agnostic_v2 is worst on TPOT and TTFT. The lesson isn't "reclaim more" — it's protect the layer that needs it. layer-aware makes that call per layer; agnostic_v2 makes the opposite bet blindly.

Methodology correction. An earlier draft ranked agnostic_v2 best at high load. That was an artifact of a hand-rolled 60-thread urllib client (GIL-bottlenecked) measured past saturation (rate 6–10), where goodput is a coin-flip at the SLO cliff. Re-measuring with the official async client below saturation reversed it. The numbers above are the trustworthy ones.

06

When each policy wins

The decision reduces to two axes: is serving prefill-bound (does isolating prefill help?), and are the time-dominant decode layers SM-insensitive (is there anything to release?).

PolicyReclaimed SMLow loadHigh load (prefill-bound)Wins when…
fused 0 strong TTFT collapses Small model / decode-bound; fast single-batch on all SMs.
co-schedule residual ~ contended No isolation guarantee; not directly measured here.
agnostic v1 44–64 (all layers) strong ≈3× fused The robust default. Prefill-bound serving where most decode layers are SM-sensitive (e.g. NemotronH).
agnostic_v2 92 (all layers, max) ok TPOT blows up Only if every decode layer tolerates 16 SMs. On both models tested the time-dominant layer is SM-sensitive, so its decode balloons past the TPOT SLO (191ms) and even hurts TTFT — the worst policy. Rarely the right bet.
layer-aware up to 92 (insensitive only) ≈ agnostic ≤ agnostic — (D) granularity Retired. Never beats agnostic on any of the four hybrids (§07). A per-layer window (~0.8 ms) is below the step granularity at which the prefill↔decode SM trade pays off, so there is no benefit to capture — only prefill contention. Ties at light load, loses under it; on Zamba2's own "ideal case" (45/54 released) it is the worst policy.

Bottom line. fused → pdmux is the large, robust win at load. Among the reservation policies, the two uniform ones are opposite bets: agnostic v1 bets no layer can be starved (reserve high) — right on both models tested; agnostic_v2 bets none needs protecting (reserve low) — wrong on both, so it's the worst. layer-aware doesn't bet: it reads each layer's sensitivity, matching agnostic v1 where nothing is releasable (NemotronH) and beating it where much is (Zamba2's TTFT). The real-engine port's value was measuring where those bets pay off — and catching that the first measurement, taken past saturation with a GIL-bound client, had them backwards.

Layer-aware, serving-validated on all four — it never wins

The whole reason this project exists was "reserve SM for sensitive layers, release it on insensitive ones." Measured directly (clean async, agnostic vs layer-aware, goodput@SLO):

layer-aware matches agnostic at light load, then loses as load rises — on every model. The case predicted as the big win (Zamba2, 45/54 layers released) is the worst: goodput 0 from rate 3 (TPOT 131ms vs agnostic's 48).

Chasing the cause took four passes (each time an experiment overturned the last guess): (A) per-span switching overhead — ~0 (release-floor at 96 keeps the switches but no starvation → identical to agnostic). (B) compute-starvation of released layers — small (~8% in isolation). (C) prefill contention — the real collapse: run the same green-ctx switching without pdmux (no concurrent prefill) and it vanishes (TPOT ≈ plain fused); the +115% only appears with prefill, because the released decode context is carved from the global pool and overlaps prefill's partition. (D) granularity — the fundamental one: a per-layer window (~0.8ms) is shorter than a concurrent prefill kernel, and kernels can't rebalance SM mid-flight, so the freed SM can't actually be captured by prefill. The prefill↔decode SM trade only pays off when the split is held stable for a whole step — which is exactly what agnostic already does. So this per-layer form captures no benefit here: below the step granularity there is no window to exploit, only contention to pay. Retired as an implementation; agnostic (pdmux) operates at the one granularity where the trade works. The general per-type form (not just the binary full/floor) was then built and swept — but, crucially, with the same uncoordinated green-ctx as the binary policy, so it pays the same contention (C). A coordinated per-type reservation (prefill given the exact complement of each layer-type's decode partition) remains unbuilt and untested; the retirement is of the buildable-today form, argued — not proven — to generalize (below).

"Isn't the tested policy a strawman?" — the graduated form, and the coordination catch

Fair objection: the retired policy is 2-level (SM-sensitive type → full, insensitive → floor 16), whereas the real idea is a per-type graduated allocation. The green-ctx layer accepts any per-type SM count, so I built it (PDMUX_LA_SM_MAP) and swept Zamba2 (in3600/out32, serving batch, clean async). The sweep exposed a subtler point than "no surplus": it pins decode to an independent green-ctx that is not the complement of pdmux's prefill partition — the same uncoordinated flaw as the binary policy (contention, pass C). The decisive control: agnostic runs decode on a coordinated pdmux pair (decode 34–54 SM · prefill the exclusive 74–54); the same 54 decode SM on an uncoordinated green-ctx costs 121 ms TPOT vs agnostic's 42 ms. So the "collapse below ~84 SM" below is coordination overhead, not a decode-SM requirement:

The surplus the objection points to is real — agnostic already reclaims it, handing prefill up to 74 of 108 SM when the decode batch is light, all coordinated. The graduated sweep only shows that a second, uncoordinated decode partition fights the first: below ~84 SM the two overlap enough to collapse decode; at 96 SM decode just overpowers the contention (and coincidentally matches agnostic). So this does not prove "no surplus" — it re-confirms pass (C): the buildable per-layer form is uncoordinated and pays contention. The genuine open question is a coordinated per-type reservation — releasing an insensitive layer's SM and handing prefill the exact complement, per window. That needs event-loop surgery (interleaving prefill chunks with decode layer-windows) and is unbuilt / untested; (D) argues prefill can't re-coordinate within a step, but that is an argument, not this measurement. Practical call: use agnostic today — the per-type refinement is unproven, not disproven. (R0b — jobs 835044–835209, results/r0b/.)

What actually holds, once you insist on serving evidence. Splitting prefill from decode (pdmux / agnostic) is the robust win on every hybrid measured — spatial and temporal, 3B to 8B. agnostic_v2 (starve decode to a floor) is the worst wherever decode has real work, confirmed in serving on both NemotronH and Granite. And layer-aware is worse than plain agnostic on all four (ties at light load, loses under it) — its case rested on decode-side micro-timing that, as Granite and Zamba2 proved, doesn't survive real batch. The clean takeaways are about the kernels (mamba sensitivity = SSD implementation, not size; attn O(L) = no-GQA), but the actionable recommendation is simply: use pdmux; don't over-starve decode; validate any per-layer refinement in serving before believing it.
